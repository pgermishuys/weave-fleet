Mission Control

Start from the person, not the plumbing. You have twenty agents running, some with helpers of their own, on five machines. Three of them need you. Today Fleet shows you one conversation at a time and a tree of rows. vNext should show you everything that's moving, what needs you, and what's done, and let you steer any agent without opening it. Where each one runs becomes a detail, not the way you find it.

3 · Two attempts, side by side

Spare machines make trying twice cheap

Ask for two attempts at a piece and Fleet runs them at the same time on two free seats, with different harnesses or models if you like. When both finish they arrive as one item in Needs you. You see the approach, the size, the tests, the time and the cost, and keep one. This is where elastic machines pay off in the UI.

Borrowed, and new

What others do, and what only Fleet can

Taken from others

Groups ordered by what you must do (Claude Code's agent view). One line that is the question when blocked, the result when done and the action while working. Space to peek, number keys to answer, replies that queue unless you say now. Helpers rolled into their parent's tile. Best-of-n as a way to start work (Cursor).

Nobody has these yet, as far as we found

Every harness in one wall: tools that cover only their own agent lose people's trust. Agents on several of your own machines in one view. Asks sorted by how much work they unblock. Attempts compared side by side after they finish, not just started together.

The complaint everyone shares

"Which agent needs me?" With five or more agents, people lose track and agents sit stalled on a question. The left column is the answer, and it's the part to get right first.

The next bottleneck

More agents means more PRs waiting for review. A later idea: a limit on pieces waiting for your review, so new work holds until you've caught up, instead of piling up.

From a research pass on 27 October 2026: Claude Code agent view docs (read), Cursor 3 changelog, GitHub mission control, the Codex app, Warp's agent notifications, and write-ups by people running five or more agents. Details for Conductor, Vibe Kanban and Devin weren't confirmed.

So the order flips: mission control first, grouped by folder until goals exist, on today's machines and today's data. It's useful the day it lands, and it tells us which plumbing to build next by what people reach for: compare, move, or more seats.

Decisions for you

1. Is mission control the home?

A new first rail item, "Fleet", that opens on mission control; Sessions stays as today, one click away. Behind the Experimental switch. recommended Or keep it as a separate screen next to Sessions.

2. Goals as the top-level unit

Group by goal when there are goals, by folder until then. A session you start by hand is a goal of one piece. recommended Or keep folders as the only grouping.

3. Fold Jobs into it

The Jobs screen on main becomes mission control's queue: waiting jobs are the dashed tiles. No separate Jobs rail item. recommended

4. First slice

Mission control on today's data: needs-you list with in-place answers, live tiles for every running session and helper on every machine, the peek with nudge. No goals, no compare yet. recommended About 2 PRs.
