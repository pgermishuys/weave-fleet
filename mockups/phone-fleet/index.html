Fleet on your phone, in Fleet's look

The same Fleet you have open on your computer, at thumb size: Inter, Fleet's surfaces and hairlines, its status glyphs, the permission and question cards, the composer and the tool rows. One design on iPhone and Android. Everything you can drag, swipe or pull still moves the way the native round did. Nothing here talks to Fleet; it's all pretend data.

Open the app mockupDesktop, today, proposed

Best on your phone. For the full effect add it to your Home Screen (Safari: Share, then Add to Home Screen; Chrome: menu, then Add to Home screen) and open "Fleet" from there: full screen, and swipe back without Safari's own gesture getting in the way.

StatusDesktop's glyphs, colour only where you're needed: WorkingNeeds youFinishedFailed

ShapeRadii 10 / 12 / 16 (desktop's 8 / 10 / 12 / 14 stepped up for 44px targets); rectangular buttons with ui/button's primary and outline variants; pills only where desktop uses them (the machine chip, Undo).

Decisions for you

1. Panel floating on the chrome, or edge to edge?

  1. Floating: a 6px rim of chrome around a rounded panel, the bar and tab bar on the chrome
  2. Edge to edge: the panel fills the width, rounded only at the top
Recommend 1. It's the single strongest "this is Fleet" cue (most of all in light: white on warm grey), and 12px of width is cheap at 390. Set Panel above to compare.

2. The ask docked in a session

  1. Compact: the command, Allow once and More… (the sheet has all three choices)
  2. Desktop's three numbered choices, docked
Recommend 1. The full card takes half the screen; compact leaves the conversation readable, and More… is the desktop card exactly.

3. Answering a question

  1. Tap an answer and it's sent (the button flips to a tick at once)
  2. As on desktop: pick, then Send answer
Recommend 1. Desktop's two steps exist for the 1–3 keys; on a phone one tap is the point of answering from the inbox. Typing your own still has its own send.

What a web app can't do natively

  • Haptics on iPhone: no API; the mockup uses the unofficial hidden-switch trick (iOS 18+). Android vibrates after your first tap.
  • Notifications on iPhone need Fleet on the Home Screen (iOS 16.4+), and no action buttons on the notification itself.
  • Swipe back in a Safari tab works from the middle of the page only; Safari owns the edge. From the Home Screen ours is the only one.
  • Keyboard: no height or animation events; the app follows visualViewport, so the composer trails the keyboard slightly on iPhone.
